=== feu_ferreol/intro #1a66f9b3
Thursday 18 November 1993, fourth floor of head office, Lyon.

The letter came with the morning post, between two flying saucers over the Vercors and a subscriber who sees the Virgin Mary in a damp stain. Karim set it aside. Brown envelope, Saint-Étienne postmark, Monday the 15th. Block capitals in ballpoint, pressed so hard the pen nearly went through the paper.

{{d:lettre_fantome|“TO THE NEWSPAPER. FOR A MONTH NOW THE FIRE HAS BEEN BACK IN THE FERRÉOL FORGE. YOU CAN SEE IT AT NIGHT FROM RUE DE LA MONTAT, RED, LIKE IN THE DAYS OF THE CASTINGS. IT IS THE FOUNDRYMAN FROM 1911. THEY LEFT HIM IN THE IRON AND CAST THE PIECE ANYWAY. HE COMES BACK EVERY TIME SOMEONE TOUCHES THE WORKS. FERRÉOL WILL NOT BE A CAR PARK. THERE WILL BE MORE DEATHS. AN OLD FERRÉOL HAND.”}}

At ten o'clock, Odile comes down with that day's Stéphanois, folded open at the local news. {{p:faure|Bernard Faure}}, 52, project officer in the planning department at the {{l:mairie|town hall}}, found dead on Wednesday morning at the foot of a staircase in the old {{l:site_ferreol|Ferréol Works}}. The {{l:commissariat|police}} have concluded it was an accidental fall. Fourteen lines, no photo.

Jean-Loup Sarrazin has read both over Odile's shoulder. “A ghost announces a death, and the death arrives. That's a page. Are we agreed?”

Paul folds the letter in four and slips it into his raincoat pocket. “Granted.”

That same evening, you set up in an empty office at the {{l:redaction|Stéphanois newsroom}}, on rue de la République. The radiator makes more noise than heat.

=== feu_ferreol/solution #265c495f
Bernard Faure was blackmailing Roger Ferrand, who was burying under new slabs the contaminated soil he was being paid to remove. On Tuesday 16 November, Faure comes to Ferréol to collect one last payment. They argue on the walkway of the forge workshop, and Ferrand pushes him. Faure survives the fall. Ferrand calls Hervé Vallenot from the watchman's hut. Vallenot arrives, sees that Faure is still breathing and refuses to call for help. He smokes four cigarillos beside him, then they stage the fall and leave. Faure dies around midnight, crawling towards the door. The next day, Marcel Roussillon has the case closed. As for the ghost's letter, it was written by Marcel Chaptal.

=== noye_ouveze/intro #b5d29953
Thursday 16 June 1994, fourth floor of head office, Lyon. It is already thirty degrees at ten in the morning, and Odile's fan is stirring the paperwork.

The letter came with the morning post. A sheet of squared exercise paper, a schoolgirl's handwriting, round and careful. Folded inside it, a page from Le Drômois of 2 June. In one corner of the page, dark red stains, like juice. Postmark: Nyons, Tuesday 14th.

{{d:lettre_anonyme|“Dear journalist of Occult Affairs. You write about ghosts, so come to Nyons. Look at the photo. The man on the right is Jacques Garcin. He drowned in the Ouvèze on 22 September 1992, his name is on the plaque in Vaison. Now he plays boules. Everyone here says it: the river gives back its dead. Chabert the miller swore he saw him swept away. The miller lied. Ask him why.”}}

The page is {{d:photo_concours|the winners' photo from the Grand Prix de la Digue}}, at the {{l:boulodrome|Nyons boules ground}}. Two men in vests, a cup between them. The caption says {{p:tardieu|Raoul Tardieu}} and {{p:royer|Lucien Royer}}. Royer turned his head just as the flash went off. You can see an ear, a cheek, a cap.

At eleven, Odile comes back up with a fax from the Valence office. A short item from that morning's Drômois. “Nyons: miller drowned in the canal.” {{p:chabert|Aimé Chabert}}, 63, owner of the {{l:moulin|oil mill}} in the Roman bridge quarter, was found at dawn against the canal grating. The {{l:gendarmerie|gendarmes}} have concluded it was an accident. Last line: “In 1992 Aimé Chabert witnessed the heroic death of {{p:garcin|Jacques Garcin}}, swept away by the Ouvèze flood while going to the aid of campers. {{p:helene|His widow, Hélène}}, lives in Nyons.”

Karim has read the letter three times. “She wrote on Tuesday. He died on Wednesday night.”

Jean-Loup Sarrazin puts his head round the door. “A river that gives back its dead. It's summer, people want a shiver on the beach. You leave tonight.” Paul folds the newspaper page in two without taking his eyes off the photo. “Granted.”

Three hours of motorway, then the road up the Eygues valley. It is dark when you arrive. The car windows are down, there is a smell of lime blossom and cut lavender, and the cicadas have not yet realised the sun has set. The {{l:agence|Le Drômois office}}, on avenue Henri-Rochier, used to be a haberdasher's. The metal shutter is half down. A fan turns on the ceiling, and on the desk they have left for you, someone has put a basket of apricots.

Gisèle Arnaud, correspondent

=== noye_ouveze/clues/gisele/text #d01d5256
{{p:gisele|Gisèle Arnaud}} has been Le Drômois's correspondent in Nyons for twenty-two years. She covers council meetings, agricultural shows, weddings and boules tournaments. She has brought a lemon cake “for the Lyon people” and she watches you eat it.

“The photo's mine. Sunday 29 May, at La Digue. It's the first time they've ever picked me up for the regional page.” She smiles, then she stops smiling. “He turned his head just as I pressed the button. I didn't do it on purpose, you have to believe me. I didn't know, about Garcin.”

Since 2 June, she says, people have talked about nothing else. In Vaison most of all. “They all lost someone, down there. And now they see their dead everywhere. {{p:rosa|Rosa Bérenger}}, one of the flood widows, swears she saw her husband last Thursday, at the market, in front of the cheese stall. She spends her days at the {{l:memorial|memorial}}, near the Roman bridge in Vaison. She says the river gives back its dead. People are starting to repeat it.”

She lowers her voice, though there is nobody else in the office. “And there's this old gentleman, at the Pension des Forts. He came in on 2 June and bought three copies of the paper. Three. He asked me exactly what time I took the photo. I told him about six. He said: no, at twelve minutes to six, look at the shadow of the plane tree.” She checked. He was right.

“His name's {{p:firmin|Achard}}, I think. He plays boules, but I've never seen him win.”

TO THE EDITORS OF LE DRÔMOIS, VALENCE · POSTED 15 JUNE 1994

Dear Sirs,

On 2 June you published the photo of a man called Lucien Royer. He is Jacques Garcin. He did not die in 1992. I lied to the gendarmes. I did not see him swept away. I found him alive, that night, up in the hills, and he asked me to say he was dead. I did. He did not go back for the caravan at the far end. He had forgotten it.

Aimé Chabert

=== noye_ouveze/clues/confrontation/button #0bb563bf

Karim's grid

=== noye_ouveze/clues/confrontation/text #41b5c879
Paul has asked Gisèle to lend you the back room of the office, where the old issues are kept. A table, four chairs, stacks of yellowed newspapers up to the ceiling. He has them come in one at a time, an hour apart, without telling them who else is coming. Karim writes everything down, word for word, on a large sketch pad.

{{p:helene|Hélène Garcin}} comes in a pale suit, her handbag on her knees, and does not sit back in the chair. {{p:royer|Lucien Royer}} comes because Tardieu told him to, cap in hand, and watches the door. {{p:firmin|Firmin Achard}} arrives exactly on time, and points it out. {{p:sandrine|Sandrine Mollet}} comes between two days of picking, her fingers red, and answers the table rather than Paul.

Paul asks them all the same question: on Wednesday night, at about ten, where were you, what did you have on you, and why were you out?

On her way out, Sandrine turns back to Karim, not to Paul. “The man I gave the boule back to, at twenty to, under the street lamp at La Digue. I'd watched him play all afternoon, from a distance. Short hair, grey. High forehead, with lines, three of them. A moustache, I think. Well. I don't know any more. I've looked at that man so much in the paper. Square chin. I didn't look him in the eye.”

Karim has made a fair copy of the statements, next to what the gendarmes' file says. He has drawn the grids. “One of them is lying. Everything he says is false. The other three are telling the truth.”

Royer's hut is at the far end of the orchard, a drystone shelter with a plank door and no lock. A camp bed, a basin, a camping stove, a 1993 Post Office calendar he has never changed.

On a shelf, an unframed photo held up by a drawing pin: two children on a pebble beach, a boy and a girl. Under the bed, a leather case. Inside, four bronze {{d:boules|lyonnaise boules}}, heavy as cobblestones, in felt compartments. On each one, engraved by hand, two initials: “J.G.”

In an iron sugar tin on the shelf, a bundle of {{d:mandats|postal orders}}, never cashed. Nineteen of them. Sender: “H. Garcin, Nyons”. Under the elastic band, a folded note in a woman's hand, very upright: “For your silence. For mine.”

Yves photographs the photo of the children, then puts it back exactly where it was, with the same drawing pin, in the same hole.

Night at Les Saules

=== noye_ouveze/clues/poursuite/text #d63d7997
It is eleven at night. Karim and Yves are crouching behind the wall of the old shower block at Camping des Saules, which no longer has a roof. The moon lights up the concrete slabs of the pitches, the twisted pipes, the bed of pebbles where the Ouvèze barely flows, so low you could cross it in your socks. The cicadas have gone quiet. You can hear the crickets, and an owl.

At twenty past eleven, a moped with no headlight. It stops at the entrance. A man walks to the far end, under what is left of the willows. He sets a glass jar on the ground and lights a candle inside it. He crouches in front of it. He is two metres from Karim.

The flame lights his face from below.

The campsite ruins

=== noye_ouveze/clues/ruines/text #525d6282
In daylight, Camping des Saules looks like a bombed town in miniature. The slabs of the numbered pitches, the roofless shower block, a rusty sign, “ACCUEIL · RECEPTION · EMPFANG”, hanging by a single bolt. The vegetation has taken over everywhere except on the slabs.

Wild flowers, poppies and lavender, cut a day or two ago. On the cross, two first names carved carefully with a knife: “Thierry · Nathalie”.

Yves looks for the pitch number. The marker post has fallen into the grass. He stands it up: 31.

Rosa Bérenger

=== noye_ouveze/clues/rosa/text #d8d923c9
{{p:rosa|Rosa Bérenger}} is sitting on the bench facing the memorial, in the shade, a promotional fan in one hand and a rosary in the other. She is sixty-eight, with an Italian accent she hasn't lost in fifty years of the Vaucluse, and a photo of her husband in her handbag, which she takes out before anyone asks.

“Marius. Forty-one years married. The river took him in the cellar, he'd gone down to fetch the wine.” She puts the photo away. “Last Thursday I saw him at the market. In front of the cheese stall. He was wearing his jacket. He looked at me, and he went away.”

She doesn't cry. She explains, patiently, as if to children. “Since the photo in the paper, everyone knows. The river gives back its dead, monsieur. It kept Garcin two years, and it gave him back. It will give back the others.”

Paul doesn't contradict her. Karim writes everything down.

“And at night there's a light at {{l:camping_saules|Camping des Saules}}. A little light, at the back. I see it from my window, I live on the Malaucène road. It's them. They come back to where they left.”

=== noye_ouveze/solution #39747918
On 22 September 1992, Jacques Garcin evacuates his Camping des Saules with the list of guests, but he has forgotten to put on it the caravan of Thierry Mollet and Nathalie Brun, who arrived the day before. They die. Garcin disappears, and his friend Aimé Chabert, the miller, swears he saw him swept away while going to the rescue. The next day, Garcin rings his wife, Hélène: “You're dead. Stay dead.” She collects 1.4 million francs in life insurance and sends him postal orders to keep him quiet. Garcin lives at Les Pilles under the name Lucien Royer. In June 1994, a photo from a competition gives him away. Sandrine Mollet, Thierry's sister, recognises him, goes and shouts at Chabert's door and writes to the Bureau. Chabert decides to tell the gendarmes everything on Thursday 16, and warns Hélène. On Wednesday 15 at 10 p.m., he sees Garcin on the bank of the canal; Garcin leaves at half past ten, and Chabert is alive. At 11.05 p.m., Hélène enters the mill with her keys, puts Chabert to sleep with her Mogadon in a glass of marc, opens the sluice gate and pushes him into the millrace. Firmin Achard saw everything from the Roman bridge, and said nothing.
